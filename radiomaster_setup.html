RadioMaster Pocket
RC Setup Guide

Drive your DonkeyCar from a RadioMaster Pocket instead of a game controller. This guide covers the Arduino Pro Micro bridge that turns the radio link into a USB joystick (wiring, firmware, checks), then the Pocket itself: ExpressLRS 3.3.2 on the radio and receiver, the receiver's CRSF settings, the Pocket's model, and the DonkeyCar controller with its arming interlock.

📻RadioMaster Pocket (ELRS)
📡RP4-TD or RP3 receiver
🔌Arduino Pro Micro
🚗DonkeyCar 5.x
01

How the RC Link Works

What each part does and what you need

DonkeyCar reads a joystick. An ExpressLRS receiver outputs a serial protocol called CRSF. The Arduino Pro Micro sits between them: it reads CRSF from the receiver on one pin and presents itself to the Raspberry Pi as a USB joystick with six axes and three buttons. DonkeyCar then treats the Pocket like any other game controller.

What You Need

PartNotes
RadioMaster Pocket, ELRS versionTakes two 18650 cells.
ExpressLRS 2.4 GHz receiverYours is a RadioMaster RP4-TD or an RP3. Either works; pick the matching target when you flash it (Section 08).
Arduino Pro Micro (ATmega32U4)Micro-USB and USB-C versions both work.
5 V BEC / buck converterPowers the receiver from the car battery (XT30). The receiver resets if it runs from the Pro Micro's 5 V pin.
Three signal wires and a USB cableReceiver → Pro Micro: signal, 5 V, ground. A USB-A cable from the Pi to the Pro Micro.
Relay module (optional)Active-high, 5 V coil. For the VESC kill switch in Section 05.
02

Wire the Receiver to the Pro Micro

Three wires between receiver and Pro Micro, power from the BEC, USB to the Pi

Do this with everything unpowered. The receiver's labels are TX, RX, 5V and GND (or G); the Pro Micro's are printed next to each pin.

FromToWhy
Receiver TXPro Micro RX1 (pin 0, sometimes labelled RXI)Transmit goes to receive. The firmware listens on Serial1, which is this pin. TX-to-TX is the most common wiring mistake and gives a dead joystick.
Receiver GNDPro Micro GND and BEC −The data signal needs a common ground between all three.
Receiver 5VBEC +5 VNot the Pro Micro's 5 V pin: the receiver draws more than it can supply and resets when it enters Wi-Fi mode.
Receiver RXNothingThe bridge never sends to the receiver, and the Pro Micro's TX pin is 5 V while the receiver's input is 3.3 V.
Pro Micro USBA USB-A port on the PiPowers the Pro Micro and carries the joystick. Use a black USB 2 port and leave the blue USB 3 ports for the camera.
Pro Micro VCC / RAWNothingUSB already powers it. Feeding the BEC in here too would put two supplies on one rail.
⚠
Check the BEC Before You Connect It
Measure the BEC's output with a meter before the receiver goes on it: it should read 5.0 V, and the XT30's polarity must match the receiver's 5V and GND pads. A buck converter set to the wrong voltage destroys the receiver silently.
03

Flash the Pro Micro

Write the prebuilt firmware from the Pi over SSH; nothing to install on your laptop

The bridge firmware is a modified build of NikitaB04/ELRSController. It reads CRSF frames from the receiver and checks each one's checksum, presents six axes and three buttons to the Pi as a USB joystick, centres everything and drops the kill relay within 250 ms of losing the radio link, and prints a status line you will use to check the whole radio path (Section 04).

You flash it from the Pi. The Pro Micro is plugged into the Pi anyway, the Pi's USB-A ports power every kind of Pro Micro, and the only tool needed is avrdude, one package. Nothing has to be installed on your laptop.

Firmware and DonkeyCar files
https://github.com/UCSD-Silberman-Classes-and-Projects/Markdown-Instructions/tree/main/Radio/pocket-bridge
# firmware.hex        the bridge firmware
# flash_promicro.sh   writes it from the Pi (this section)
# my_joystick.py      the DonkeyCar controller (Section 10)
# rc_probe.py         the bench test for it (Section 10)
# source/             the complete source, for anyone who wants to change the firmware
1
Plug the Pro Micro Into the Pi
Use one of the Pi's black USB 2 ports and a cable that carries data. The board's LED lights. Most boards arrive with a demo sketch, so the Pi already lists it as an Arduino:
Pi terminal
lsusb | grep -i -E 'arduino|sparkfun'
# ID 2341:8036 Arduino SA Leonardo        (or 1b4f:9206 SparkFun Pro Micro; either is fine)
2
Copy the Files to the Pi
From the folder you downloaded, copy the four files to the Pi's home directory. Replace XX with your car's number, as in the Raspberry Pi guide.
Your laptop terminal
scp firmware.hex flash_promicro.sh my_joystick.py rc_probe.py pi@ucsdrobocar-148-XX.local:~/
3
Install avrdude and Flash
Close anything that has the joystick open first (jstest, manage.py). The script finds the Pro Micro, resets it into its bootloader, writes the firmware and reads it back to verify. The board restarts on its own a few seconds later.
Pi terminal
sudo apt-get install -y avrdude
bash ~/flash_promicro.sh ~/firmware.hex
# resetting /dev/serial/by-id/usb-Arduino_LLC_Arduino_Leonardo…-if00 into the bootloader
# bootloader port: /dev/serial/by-id/usb-Arduino_LLC_Arduino_Leonardo-if00
# avrdude: 11884 bytes of flash written
# avrdude: 11884 bytes of flash verified
# avrdude done.  Thank you.
4
Confirm the New Firmware Is Running
Within a few seconds the board is back as a joystick with 3 buttons. Section 04 has the check and the status line that tells you the rest.
ℹ
Brand-New or Blank Board?
A Pro Micro with no sketch on it has no serial port, so the script cannot reset it and says so. Put it into the bootloader by hand: briefly connect RST to GND twice within a second. The bootloader shows up as a serial port for about 8 seconds and the script, which is waiting, flashes it.
04

Verify the Bridge on the Pi

Confirm the Pi sees the joystick, and meet the status line you will use for every later check

These checks work before the Pocket is set up; the joystick simply reads zero until the radio link is up.

Pi terminal
lsusb | grep -i leonardo
# Bus 003 Device 002: ID 2341:8036 Arduino SA Leonardo (CDC ACM, HID)

ls -l /dev/input/by-id/
# usb-Arduino_LLC_Arduino_Leonardo_HIDFF-if02-joystick -> ../js0   (the number can change; always use this name)

jstest --normal /dev/input/by-id/usb-Arduino_LLC_Arduino_Leonardo_HIDFF-if02-joystick
# Joystick (Arduino LLC Arduino Leonardo) has 6 axes (X, Y, Rx, Ry, Throttle, Rudder)
# and 3 buttons (Btn0, Btn1, Btn2).      ← 3 buttons confirms the Section 03 firmware is running
⚠
Never Assume js0
Joystick numbers follow the order devices come up at boot. A built-in Logitech receiver or a second controller can push the Pro Micro to js1. Always use the /dev/input/by-id/… name: it is the same on every car running this firmware.

The Status Line

The firmware prints one line a second on its USB serial port with everything you need to diagnose the link: how many bytes and valid frames arrived, how many failed the checksum, whether the link is up, and the raw value of each radio channel. Reading it is harmless; the bridge keeps working as a joystick at the same time.

Pi terminal
p=/dev/serial/by-id/usb-Arduino_LLC_Arduino_Leonardo_HIDFF-if00
stty -F $p 115200 raw -echo && timeout 5 cat $p
# t=84000 loops=72000 bytes=3900 frames=145 crc=0 link=1 ch=1016,174,1008,992,191,997,1792,191,997,997,997,997
FieldHealthy valueMeaning
framesabout 110–150 at a 150 Hz packet rateValid CRSF channel frames in the last second. 0 means the receiver's output is not CRSF at 115200, or the wire is wrong.
bytesabout 3000–4000Bytes received. Bytes without frames means the format is wrong (SBUS, or the wrong baud rate).
crclow and steadyFrames rejected by the checksum since power-up. A fast-climbing count points at a bad connection or the wrong packet rate.
link10 once no valid frame has arrived for 250 ms: the failsafe is active.
chcentre ≈ 992, range 172–1811; switches 191 / 997 / 1792Raw channels CH1–CH12 as the receiver sends them, on ExpressLRS's own scale (the same for every radio, not a Pocket setting). The fastest way to see what the Pocket is doing.
loopstens of thousandsMain-loop passes per second. A tiny number means the firmware is stalling.
🚨
Never Open That Port at 1200 Baud
1200 baud is the "reset into the bootloader" signal for this board. Any other speed is fine for reading the status line.
05

Kill-Switch Relay

A hardware stop that does not depend on the Pi, wired so that losing the Pro Micro stops the car
Not yet verified on a car

The VESC has a kill-switch input: with Kill Switch Mode set to ADC2 Low, pulling its ADC2 pin to ground stops the motor regardless of what the Pi sends. The Pro Micro drives a relay into that pin from its pin 8. In this firmware pin 8 is HIGH only while the arm switch is on and the radio link is up. Everything else, including a Pro Micro with no power, no USB or crashed firmware, leaves the relay at rest, so at rest must mean "kill". That is what the wiring below does.

Relay contactConnect toState
COMVESC ADC2The pin the VESC watches
NO (closed when energised)VESC 3.3 V (use 5 V only if your VESC's ADC inputs are rated for it; check its pinout)Armed and linked: ADC2 high, motor allowed
NC (closed at rest)VESC GNDDisarmed, link lost, or Pro Micro dead: ADC2 low, motor killed
Module VCC / GND / INPro Micro VCC, GND, pin 8Use a relay module (it has the driver transistor and diode), and an active-high one: an active-low module reverses all of this.

The ADC2, 3.3 V and GND pins are on the VESC's COMM (or UART) connector, not on the servo or motor connectors. On many VESCs it is a small JST socket or a row of pads under the heat-shrink, labelled next to the pins.

1
Set the VESC
In VESC Tool, under App Settings → General, set Kill Switch Mode to ADC2 Low and write the app configuration.
2
Meter Check Before the VESC Is Connected
With the relay wired to the Pro Micro but not to the VESC, measure COM against GND. It should read high only while the Pocket is on and the arm switch is on, and drop to 0 V when you disarm, switch the Pocket off, or unplug the Pro Micro's USB. If it reads high at rest, the module is active-low or NO/NC are swapped.
3
Test With the Wheels Off the Ground
Connect COM to ADC2, spin the motor gently under DonkeyCar, then disarm: the motor must stop. Then switch the Pocket off, and then unplug the Pro Micro: the motor must stop both times.
06

Before You Flash the Pocket

The right radio, charged cells, the Configurator, and the class settings
1
Charge the Cells
The Pocket runs on two 18650 cells and charges them itself over its USB-C port with the radio off. Flat cells make the radio switch itself off a few seconds after power-up, with a low-battery alarm first. Leave it charging before a flashing session; flashing and Wi-Fi mode both draw a lot.
2
Install the ExpressLRS Configurator
Download it from the link below. On an Apple Silicon Mac take the arm64 .dmg, on an Intel Mac the x64 one; if macOS blocks the first launch, allow it under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. The Configurator's own version does not matter; you choose the firmware version inside it.
Configurator downloads
https://github.com/ExpressLRS/ExpressLRS-Configurator/releases
3
Know Your Team's Radio Settings
Your binding phrase is 148-T followed by your team number, for example 148-T7. The radio and the receiver must carry exactly the same phrase: it is what pairs them, and it is why your radio ignores every other team's car. For updates, both devices join the Wi-Fi network UCSDRoboCar (password UCSDrobocars2018).
ℹ
One Phrase per Team
ExpressLRS radios that share a binding phrase interfere with each other when they are on at the same time, and a receiver takes commands from any radio that has its phrase. One phrase per team keeps the track clean and your car yours. Leave Model Match off in the ExpressLRS tools; it is only needed when phrases are shared.

Settings You Will Enter

These are the Configurator's Device options for both devices. You will enter them in Sections 07 and 08.

07

Flash the Pocket

ExpressLRS 3.3.2 on the radio's internal module, over USB, then the packet rate
1
Plug In the Top USB-C Port With the Radio On
The Pocket has two USB-C ports; the top one carries data. A menu appears on the radio's screen as soon as the cable goes in.
2
Choose USB Serial (VCP) on the Radio
Scroll with the roller and press it to select USB Serial (VCP). This option is not in the ExpressLRS tools page; it is EdgeTX's USB prompt. If no prompt appears, set SYS → Hardware → USB Mode to Ask and replug.
3
Check That Your Laptop Sees It
The Pocket appears as a USB serial port: /dev/cu.usbmodem… on macOS, /dev/ttyACM0 on Linux, a COM port on Windows. The Configurator lists it under Manual serial device selection. If nothing appears, see Troubleshooting.
Your laptop terminal (macOS / Linux)
ls /dev/cu.usbmodem* /dev/ttyACM* 2>/dev/null
# macOS: /dev/cu.usbmodem00000000001B1    Linux: /dev/ttyACM0    ("Radiomaster Pocket Serial Port")
4
Flash in the Configurator
Enter the radio column of the settings table in Section 06. Under Manual serial device selection pick the port above (automatic selection also works when the radio is the only serial device). Click FLASH and leave the radio on and connected until it reports success.
5
Check the Version and Set the Packet Rate
Open SYS → Tools → ExpressLRS. The version is shown at the bottom of the page. On the same page set Packet Rate to 150Hz. Leave the D, F and Full modes alone: they push data out of the receiver faster than the 115200 baud wire to the Pro Micro can carry, and the bridge starts dropping frames. 150 Hz is many times faster than DonkeyCar's control loop and gives the longest range.
✓
Already on 3.3.2?
If the version at the bottom of the ExpressLRS page already reads 3.3.2 and the radio binds to your receiver (Section 08), the radio does not need flashing. Flash it when the binding phrase is wrong or unknown.
08

Flash & Configure the Receiver

3.3.2 over Wi-Fi, then the two settings the bridge depends on: CRSF at 115200
Critical

The Configurator cannot build and flash a receiver over Wi-Fi in one go on a laptop with a single Wi-Fi interface: building needs the internet, and the receiver's hotspot has none. So you build first on your normal network, then upload the file from the receiver's own web page.

1
Build the Receiver Firmware
On your normal Wi-Fi, enter the receiver column of the table in Section 06, with WIFI as the flashing method and the same binding phrase as the radio. Click BUILD, not FLASH, and note where it saves the file: firmware.bin for an RP4-TD, firmware.bin.gz for an RP3.
2
Put the Receiver Into Wi-Fi Mode
Power the receiver (from the BEC, Section 02) with the Pocket off. After 60 seconds without a radio its LED starts blinking fast: it is now a Wi-Fi hotspot. If the receiver is already bound to your radio, you can instead open SYS → Tools → ExpressLRS → WiFi Connectivity → Enable Rx WiFi on the Pocket. That entry is on the main ExpressLRS page, not under Other Devices.
3
Upload the File
Join the Wi-Fi network ExpressLRS RX (password expresslrs; your laptop loses internet while connected), open http://10.0.0.1, go to the UPDATE tab and upload the file. Wait for the success message. If the receiver has already joined UCSDRoboCar, it does not start its own hotspot while that network is in range: connect your laptop to the same network and find it in the Configurator's Network Devices list (WIFI flashing method), or ask your instructor for its address.
4
Set the Serial Protocol to CRSF and the Baud to 115200
Still on the receiver's page: on the MODEL tab set Serial Protocol to CRSF (plain, not Inverted) and click that tab's Save. Then on the OPTIONS tab set UART baud to 115200 and click its Save. Reload the page and confirm both. Reflashing resets these, so repeat this step after any future flash unless RCVR_UART_BAUD was in the build.
5
Bind and Check
Rejoin your normal Wi-Fi. Turn the Pocket on first, then power-cycle the receiver so it starts with the new settings. Its LED should go solid, and the ExpressLRS page on the Pocket shows a C for connected. Switch the Pocket off and the LED should start blinking within a second or two: that is your receiver.
🚨
CRSF, Not SBUS
Leave the receiver's Serial Protocol on CRSF; never choose SBUS or Inverted SBUS. The bridge firmware reads CRSF frames at 115200 baud. ExpressLRS sends SBUS at a fixed 100000 baud with the signal inverted, so with SBUS or Inverted SBUS selected the Pro Micro decodes nothing and jstest shows every axis stuck at −32767. The default CRSF speed is 420000, which the Pro Micro cannot read either; that is what the 115200 setting is for.
⚠
UART Baud Shows 100000 and Cannot Be Changed?
The web page locks the baud field at 100000 while the Serial Protocol is SBUS or Inverted SBUS. Change the protocol to CRSF on the MODEL tab first; the field unlocks at once. Also, the receiver only applies a protocol change after a restart, so power-cycle it before you test.
ℹ
Which Receiver Do You Have?
Once bound, the Pocket names it under SYS → Tools → ExpressLRS → Other Devices: RM RP4-TD 2400 or RM RP3. The Configurator target must match; flashing the wrong one fails or leaves the receiver dark. The receiver's Wi-Fi page also shows its target name at the top.
09

Set Up the Pocket Model

Stick calibration, switch types, and the mixes that put each switch on a channel

The radio decides what each channel carries. Two settings on a fresh or borrowed Pocket are often wrong, and each produces a car that does nothing while the link looks healthy: an uncalibrated stick sends a constant value, and a switch with no hardware type is ignored by every mix. Do these three steps in order, then check with the status line.

1
Calibrate the Sticks
Open SYS → Hardware → Calibration and follow the prompts: sticks centred, then each stick to its full extent in every direction. Symptom when this is wrong: CH1–CH4 in the status line sit near 1800 whatever you do with the sticks.
2
Give Each Switch a Hardware Type
In SYS → Hardware, scroll to the switches. Each one you intend to use must have a type; NONE means it always reads 0. Set the type by how the switch behaves in your hand:
Switch on the PocketBehaviourType
SA (bumper with an indicator LED)Two positions, stays where put2POS
SB (rocker)Three positions3POS
SCThree positions3POS
SD (bumper)Two positions2POS
SE (large pad)Springs back when releasedTOGGLE
3
Add the Switch Mixes
Open MDL → MIXES. CH1–CH4 are already Ail, Ele, Thr, Rud at weight 100; leave them. Scroll to CH5, long-press the roller (a short press only selects the line), choose Edit, and set Source to SA by flipping SA; the radio picks up the switch you move. Leave the Switch field empty: that field only decides when a mix is active, and a mix with no source outputs 0 whatever the switch does. Weight stays 100. Repeat: CH6 ← SB, CH7 ← SC, CH8 ← SD, CH9 ← SE.

What Each Control Does

This is the layout the DonkeyCar controller in Section 10 expects. The Pocket's left stick does not spring back up and down, which is why it is the throttle: you set a speed and it holds.

ControlChannelJoystick input on the PiDoes
Right stick, left–rightCH1axis RxSteering
Left stick, up–downCH2axis RyThrottle amount: bottom = stopped, top = full. Holds its position
SA (LED on)CH5button 0Arm. LED off = disarmed: throttle 0, user mode, no recording
SBCH6axis RudderDirection: forward / neutral / reverse
SCCH7axis ThrottleDrive mode: top = user (you drive), middle = autopilot steers, bottom = full autopilot

Check It

With the Pro Micro on the Pi and the receiver bound, read the status line from Section 04 while you move one control at a time. Switches step between 191, 997 and 1792; sticks sweep from about 172 to 1811 with centre near 992. Those numbers are ExpressLRS's channel scale, the same on every radio; a stick that rests a little off 992 is mechanical centre and trim. If SA's LED-on position reads 191 instead of 1792, change the CH5 mix weight to −100.

Pi terminal — SA on, left stick at the bottom, SB middle, SC top, SD off
#                                                   CH1  CH2 CH3  CH4 CH5  CH6 CH7  CH8 CH9
t=90000 loops=71000 bytes=3000 frames=110 crc=0 link=1 ch=1016,174,1008,992,1792,997,1792,191,997,997,997,997
10

Connect It to DonkeyCar

The custom controller, its arming interlock, a bench test, then the first drive with the wheels up

DonkeyCar loads a custom controller from ~/mycar/my_joystick.py when CONTROLLER_TYPE is "custom". The file in the download folder maps the Pocket as in the table above, and adds two interlocks that a game-controller mapping does not need, because the throttle stick holds its position and DonkeyCar ignores where the sticks were when it started.

ℹ
The Arming Interlock
To arm: throttle stick at the bottom, SC at the top (user), then SA on. Arming takes about half a second. If the stick is not at the bottom or SC is not on user, arming is refused and stays refused until you cycle SA off and on. The log tells you why.

Direction changes with SB only engage when the stick is at the bottom; neutral applies at once. So the motor is never thrown from forward to reverse under power.

Disarmed (SA off, or the radio link lost) means throttle 0, user mode and no recording, whatever the sticks say.
1
Copy the Controller Into Your Car Folder
Pi terminal (files copied to the Pi first)
cp my_joystick.py rc_probe.py ~/mycar/
2
Set the Controller in myconfig.py
Replace your existing controller block in ~/mycar/myconfig.py with this. There is no JOYSTICK_DEVICE_FILE line: DonkeyCar does not pass it to a custom controller, so my_joystick.py holds the device path itself.
~/mycar/myconfig.py
CONTROLLER_TYPE = "custom"
USE_JOYSTICK_AS_DEFAULT = True
JOYSTICK_MAX_THROTTLE = 0.5
JOYSTICK_THROTTLE_DIR = 1.0     # left stick up = more throttle (bottom = stopped; SB sets direction)
JOYSTICK_STEERING_SCALE = 1.0   # -1.0 if the car steers the wrong way
JOYSTICK_DEADZONE = 0.05        # bottom 5% of the throttle stick counts as stopped
AUTO_RECORD_ON_THROTTLE = False # SD enables recording instead
3
Bench-Test the Mapping Without the Motor
rc_probe.py runs the controller alone, with no camera and no motor, and prints a line whenever steering, throttle, mode, recording, armed or direction changes. Work through the controls: steer, try to arm with the stick up (refused), arm properly, push the throttle up, change direction, flip SD, disarm.
Pi terminal
cd ~/mycar && ~/env/bin/python rc_probe.py 90
#   49261ms angle=+0.00 throttle=+0.00 mode=user recording=False armed=True direction=+0
#   80951ms angle=+0.00 throttle=+0.50 mode=user recording=False armed=True direction=+1
4
First Drive, Wheels Off the Ground
Car on a stand, wheels clear, battery connected (the VESC only appears with it). Pocket on, receiver LED solid, SA off, throttle stick at the bottom, SC at the top, SB in the middle, SD off. Then start DonkeyCar and run through the checks below in order.
Pi terminal
cd ~/mycar && ~/env/bin/python manage.py drive
#DoExpect
1Disarmed: push the throttle stick up, then back downThe wheels do not turn
2Right stick left and rightFront wheels turn the matching way (if not, JOYSTICK_STEERING_SCALE = -1.0)
3SA onLog: Armed: neutral
4SB forward, stick up slightly, then back to the bottomWheels turn slowly forwards, then stop
5SB reverse, stick up slightly, then back downWheels turn backwards, then stop
6SB forward, stick up, then SA offWheels stop at once
7Re-arm, stick up, then switch the Pocket offWheels stop within about half a second, and do not restart when the Pocket comes back on
8Ctrl-CDonkeyCar exits
⚠
The Throttle Stick Does Not Spring Back
Letting go of the Pocket does not stop the car. Your stops are SA, switching the Pocket off, and the relay once it is wired. Keep VESC_MAX_SPEED_PERCENT low (0.1 is a sensible start) until you have used all of them on the stand, and never arm with the wheels on the ground until you have.
